# Work metrics: reference vs ours

Generated by `scripts/compare-frames.mjs` + `scripts/metrics-report.mjs` (raw values in
`docs/screens/metrics/*.json`).

**What is measured.** Reference: the recording's Work descent, 26–51 s (100 frames at 4 fps),
centre-cropped to 16:10, 1440×900. Ours: production build, desktop full tier, 1440×900, DPR 1,
a fixed scripted wheel scroll through all six cards (~50 frames at 4 fps). Both sides go
through the same H.264 step (`scripts/h264.mjs`, 0.8 Mb/s at 4 fps ≈ the reference's bits
per frame). 8% is cropped from every edge, and the cards are masked out (ours: the cards'
projected corners exported during the recording; reference: hand-drawn quads, one keyframe
per second, `docs/reference/work-masks-hand/quads.json`). Masks are shown in
`metrics/masks-reference.jpg` and `metrics/masks-ours-baseline.jpg`. Only the column and
the air are measured: ~31% of the cropped reference frame, ~59% of ours (our cards cover less).

- EMPTY AREA: % of 16×16 tiles with luminance std dev < 0.015 and mean < 0.12 (0–1).
- SPARKLE: 5×5 local maxima above the frame's 98th percentile and ≥ 0.25 above their 9×9
  mean, per megapixel measured.
- Target: within ±15% of the reference.

## Still outside ±15% after three tuning passes (open points)
- Mean luminance +19%, contrast +22%, p5 +24%, p95 +24%: our measured area includes much
  more of the lit column than theirs (their cards cover more of the frame; 59% vs 31% of the
  cropped frame is measured), and our beads and column glints are a little brighter.
- EMPTY AREA −29% (36% vs 51%): same cause in part (the column is never empty), and our air
  is evenly sprinkled where theirs leaves larger black areas. Emptying it further would
  contradict the brief (Work must feel full); left open.
- SPARKLE ~3× the reference: on our column (the inner glitter, kept as asked, and the facet
  glints); theirs sits mostly in the pockets. See PROGRESS.md, "Decisions I made".
- Hue distance 0.18 (target ≤ 0.15): ours still has a little more azure (210–240°) and less
  blue-violet (240–270°) than theirs.
